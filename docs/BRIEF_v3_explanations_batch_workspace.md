# Brief v3: explanation quality, batch analysis and an analysis workspace

Source: Arunabh's product brief of 30 Oct 2026, plus his follow-up on Play it out. This is a faithful summary; the plan is in `docs/PLAN_v3.md`.

## Ground rules
- Improve **rook-i.e. mistakes**; keep everything that works (username import, multi-PGN import, in-browser engine at several depths, eval graph, classifications and accuracy, level-adjusted explanations, game summary and phases, critical moments, retries, the thinking question, personal puzzles, spaced repetition, recurring-error history, local storage, export/import/delete, provenance labels Engine / Formula / Rule-based / Estimate).
- Local-first privacy. No AI or paid calls. No competitor branding. Minimalist UI with progressive disclosure.
- Build depth and trust, not breadth. The bar: every chess claim can show where it came from, interruptions recover cleanly, and analysis turns into learning the player keeps.

## Workstream 1: explanation quality
- **Pipeline:** validate position → engine analysis of the played move and candidates → structured facts → mechanism → lesson → level-appropriate prose → validate the prose → fall back to a concrete line when confidence is low. The engine decides chess truth, structured logic decides what happened, language only explains verified facts. Prose may never invent threats, captures, defenders, attackers, material outcomes, motifs, mates, plans or reasons.
- **Truth layers kept separate:** engine facts (evals, best move, PV, mate distance, acceptable alternatives); board facts (legal moves, material, attacked/defended, checks/captures/threats, hanging/overloaded, pins/forks/skewers, structure, king safety, mobility, passed pawns, files); formula outputs (classification, accuracy, moment score, phase score, skill estimate); educational interpretation (shown as interpretation, e.g. "threat recognition", "this was tempting because…").
- **Explanation record** (machine-readable, all prose traces to it): position (FEN, side to move, phase), played move (SAN, UCI, legal), best moves with PV, eval before/after and win-chance loss, materialOutcome {netChange, lost, exchanged, won}, verifiedEvents, motifs with confidence, playerResponse, recommendedLesson, confidence.
- **Material ledger** for every cited line: capture vs equal exchange vs temporary sacrifice vs genuine sacrifice vs forced recapture vs net gain vs positional sacrifice vs "queen exchange that then wins a piece". Never "wins the queen" when queens were traded. Check every material claim against the final position of the line.
- **Sections for important mistakes:** What happened (concrete, no jargon) / Why it failed (precise mechanism) / Why it was tempting (only when board facts or the player's answer support it, phrased "A plausible reason…") / Why the recommended move works (the problem it solves, not "better eval") / What to notice next time (reusable cue) / Pattern (one primary concept, optionally one secondary).
- **Thinking questionnaire:** didn't notice the threat; noticed but misjudged; stopped calculating early; assumed a recapture; focused on my own attack; thought it was defended; didn't consider another candidate; moved too quickly; low on time; don't remember; something else. Each maps to a learning category (threat awareness, evaluation/piece safety, calculation depth, forcing-move discipline, opponent-threat awareness, decision process/time management…). No vague "Partly": say which part matched and what else was going on.
- **Retries accept several good moves**, graded Best / Fully acceptable / Playable / Doesn't solve / Illegal. Bands depend on level, severity, only-move situations, win% gap, mate preservation and material. Say "This works; the engine slightly prefers Bd3, but yours solves the same problem."
- **No shallow phrasing** ("strong because it gives check", "eval decreased", "engine prefers", "punishes the error", "no tactic to name; follow the line"). Name the consequence.
- **Confidence** from depth stability, PV agreement, material clarity, motif confidence, line length and near-equal moves. Low confidence: no motif name, say it is mainly positional, show the line and board changes, invite exploration.
- **Levels change reasoning depth**, not only words: beginner one idea, short lines, checks/captures/threats; intermediate motifs, candidates, short trees, plan comparisons; advanced move order, several candidates, prophylaxis, trade-offs, longer lines, engine uncertainty.
- **Regression corpus** (FEN, played move, accepted alternatives, expected material outcome, required facts, forbidden claims, primary concept, level text) covering hanging pieces, equal exchanges, deflection, overload, pin, fork, skewer, zwischenzug, quiet defence, missed mate, false and genuine sacrifice, multiple acceptable moves, positional mistakes, endgame transitions, tablebase positions, promotion, en passant, castling legality, repetition and stalemate.
- **Automated invariants** fail on: illegal move; captured piece still on the board; queen "won" when exchanged; material contradicting the final position; mate claim not in the engine result; wrong piece on a named square; wrong side to move; nonexistent attacker/defender; "forced" move with alternatives; fake sacrifice; recommended move not solving the stated problem; prose contradicting the PV; diagnosis contradicting the selected answer.
- **Acceptance:** every factual sentence traceable; material passes the ledger; at least three acceptable alternatives recognised when applicable; explanations stable between Standard and Deep; no unexplained advanced terms for beginners; never claims to know what the player thought; explicit low-confidence fallback; corpus covers all supported motifs; human reviewers approve a benchmark set; users can report an explanation as incorrect / unclear / too advanced (stored locally, exportable).

## Workstream 2: background and batch analysis
- **Flow:** "Analyse my recent games" with last 5 / 10 / 25, since last analysis, or manual selection, and an estimate first (positions, time, workload, storage, depth).
- **Persistent queue** (IndexedDB) with statuses Waiting / Fetching / Validating / Analysing / Generating lessons / Saving / Complete / Paused / Failed / Cancelled. Shows current game, done/total, current position, time left, depth, pause/resume, readable failures. Survives navigation, refresh, worker failure and network loss. Failures don't stop the queue; cancelling is safe.
- **Priority:** recent losses → draws from a winning position → wins with big blunders → longer rapid/classical games → games with real middlegames → tiny resignations/abandonments last. Skip aborted, in-progress, unsupported variants, trivially short games and exact duplicates. User can override.
- **Resources:** engine in Web Workers; dynamic/reduced threads on mobile; yield between positions; clean cancel; release engine memory; optional "only while this tab is open" and "pause on battery". Modes Light (low depth/nodes, one worker, mobile/large batches), Standard (default), Deep (selected games only, with a warning).
- **Two passes:** screen every position with a modest budget (swings, mate transitions, material changes, candidate mistakes, missed wins); verify only critical positions deeper (best move, played-move eval, alternatives, PV, material, motif, classification stability). Only verified positions become lessons or puzzles.
- **Cache** keyed by FEN + engine version + net version + search config + depth/nodes + MultiPV; reuse across transpositions, repeats, reanalysis and duplicates, never across incompatible configs. When depth changes, keep old results and re-run only what needs more confidence.
- **Incremental saves** after each position/game (metadata, engine config, evals, verification status, moments, explanations, puzzles, timestamps, versions); resume from the last verified position.
- **Fair play:** only completed games (result + end time / API says finished). Show "Analysis is available only for completed games. The engine will never run on your active game."
- **Insights:** aggregate once per game; minimum evidence (1–2 games anecdote, 3–5 possible, 6–10 emerging, 10+ stronger), normalised by opportunity; each shows sample size, period, confidence, supporting games and positions, practice, and trend. Keep small-sample caution.
- **Batch summary:** games analysed, new puzzles, most common error, most valuable improvement, games needing review, failures. No system notifications without permission.
- **Privacy note:** games come from public archives; the engine runs locally; reviews and progress stay in the browser; clearing it removes them; JSON export is the backup; any sync later is opt-in.

## Workstream 3: analysis workspace
- **Open from** any reviewed move, critical moment, puzzle, insight, pasted PGN or FEN, manual setup, an imported game or a shared link, keeping position, orientation and line.
- **Board:** forward/back/start/end, variation tree (branch, promote, delete, undo/redo), flip, legal-move checks, arrows and highlights, comments, named positions, copy FEN, copy/download PGN, multi-PGN import, keyboard and touch. The tree marks game line, engine lines, user lines and imported annotations; engine suggestions never overwrite user lines.
- **Engine:** on/off, depth or time, fixed nodes, MultiPV 1–5 (defaults: beginner 1, intermediate 2–3, advanced configurable), eval bar, arrows, threat line, progress, version, stop/restart, resource controls.
- **Ask the position** (structured): threat, attacked pieces, undefended pieces, checks/captures/threats, why the engine prefers a move, why my move fails, candidates, after this capture, who benefits from an exchange, main weakness, plans, play it — all through the same grounded pipeline.
- **Compare moves** table: eval, win%, material outcome, forcing reply, pieces improved, threats created, risks, main concept.
- **Training:** save any position or branch as a personal puzzle; play from the position (either colour, strength, hint, restart, compare afterwards).
- **Later phases (not this slice):** opening explorer with sourced stats, repertoire and flashcards, 7-piece tablebase, diagnostic overlays, local studies, shareable read-only analysis that excludes private data, optional encrypted sync.
- **Accessibility:** keyboard, screen-reader board descriptions, colour-blind-safe markers, reduced motion, focus management, large touch targets, explanations not relying on colour or arrows alone.

## Follow-up from Arunabh: Play it out → review and compare
1. After a Play it out game ends or is stopped, offer **Review this game**: the same review pipeline (classifications, graph, grounded explanations, critical moments) on the played-out continuation, saved locally and linked to the source game and moment.
2. **Record take-backs**: every attempt or branch (including moves taken back) stays in the variation tree, so errors and corrections are visible.
3. **Compare with original**: original line vs replay (and take-back branches) from the shared position; highlight divergence points; for each side, mistakes and the win-chance path (both curves on one graph); explain each divergence in grounded terms (win% before/after, what the new move did differently via board facts and the material ledger, and whether the same concept category of error repeated or was fixed). Example: "You diverged at 16...Qc7 → 16...Bf6. In the original you lost the bishop two moves later; here you kept material and your win chance rose from 60% to 100%. You still slipped once at 19...a4 (−29%), the same piece-safety pattern."
4. Minimal: one Compare entry point, a synced step-through of both lines, and mistakes from both lines listed together aligned by move number. Tests for alignment, divergence detection, take-back capture and comparison-fact invariants; verify live on desktop and phone.

## Required first output, then the slice
Before coding: audit, reusable components, data-model changes, evidence schema, worker/queue architecture, workspace IA, risks, and a milestone plan (each milestone: acceptance criteria, chess-correctness tests, performance tests, accessibility checks, failure/recovery, privacy, affected screens). Then ship the first vertical slice: batch queue → deeper verification → fact-checked explanations → open any lesson in the workspace → save a discovered variation as a puzzle; corpus and invariant tests; checked against native Stockfish; deploy, verify live on desktop and phone (refresh mid-batch then resume; UI stays responsive), commit and push.
